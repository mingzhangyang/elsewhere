# Sky moments: SM0-B time and science fixture register

Status: **SM1 time semantics covered; SM2-A geometry tested; SM2-B synthetic selection policy tested; SM2-C synthetic and in-project real HYG/astronomy snapshots added; independent SM2-C scene reference checks pending**. Based on `main` at `0e76c7f` (November 22, 2026). Inputs are fictional, not personal records. This register complements the human-session script in [`SKY-MOMENT-TEST.md`](SKY-MOMENT-TEST.md); it is not a report of completed scientific validation.

## Frozen rules for SM1-A

- The event place supplies WGS84 latitude/longitude (degrees north/east), elevation (metres) and an **IANA** time zone. Never infer the event zone from the device or the weather provider.
- `exact` records a supplied Gregorian date and `HH:mm` local clock; `reference` records a separate reference clock and its origin. The reference defaults to **22:00 local** and stays a reference when changed. Seconds in the input are zero; stored UTC uses ISO milliseconds.
- A spring gap is an error, not an automatic shift. A fall fold exposes **both** ordered UTC instants with their real offsets, including historical seconds; no default choice. A new place preserves the local date and clock but clears any prior choice.
- The **UTC instant**, not the civil year, must be between `1900-01-01T00:00:00.000Z` and `2100-12-31T23:59:59.999Z`, inclusive. Persist UTC, the original civil date/clock, IANA zone and the offset used. When reading a future saved artwork, verify `UTC + saved offset = saved wall time`; do **not** silently reinterpret a self-consistent record if the device tzdb has since changed.
- SM2 will use the existing apparent-altitude convention, draw the full **above-horizon hemisphere**, put **north at top, east at left**, use a circular azimuthal-equidistant projection, and ignore daylight/weather concealment only in the _diagram_ (never the interactive explorer). The initial limiting magnitude is 6.0; star patterns are off by default. The planned fixed layout, restrained two-palette choice and 1200 × 1500 PNG are SM3 goals, not implemented here.

These rules follow [`SKY-MOMENTS-PLAN.md`](SKY-MOMENTS-PLAN.md) §§5–6, 8, 10–11 and agree with [`SCIENCE.md`](SCIENCE.md) and [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md). No conflicting rule was found requiring edits to those documents.

## SM2-B object-selection evidence (November 22, 2026)

`tests/skyChartSelection.test.ts` exercises the pure selector on small **synthetic** already-refracted altitude/azimuth inputs. The apparent **center** horizon is inclusive at exactly 0 degrees for all four kinds; negative centers are excluded; zenith 90 degrees is included. Stellar magnitude 6.0 passes the default inclusive limit, while 6.000001 fails; legal custom limits [-5, 20] are tested. Sun, Moon and all five planets ignore that stellar threshold, their `visible` flags and `geometricAltitude`. All selected objects can be passed unchanged to SM2-A and produce finite unit-disc positions.

Daytime with Sun altitude 50 degrees, and city pollution under a high fully illuminated Moon, both keep a synthetic above-horizon star in the **memorial** result while the existing explorer model assigns it zero visibility. Weather and device context are not inputs. Illegal/non-finite positions, magnitudes, options, color indices, missing data, repeated IDs and unready-catalog input are explicitly rejected. Reordering inputs or changing the observer hemisphere does not change the coordinate policy. The input contracts require a boolean readiness assertion: an **unready** catalog throws `SkyChartCatalogNotReadyError`; a **ready** catalog with zero selected matches can return an empty object array. SM2-C is responsible for confirming catalog/body completeness and for loading state.

**Evidence limits:** S03 is **selection-boundary policy tested**, not independently checked astronomical horizon positions for New York/Sydney. S04 is **daytime selection policy tested**, not yet a calculated real-sky snapshot or independent ephemeris match. S01/S02 retain their SM2-A analytic geometry status. S05-S07 remain **pending**. No historical or research-grade accuracy claim follows from these tests; crescent orientation, chart snapshots, resource orchestration, rendering and safety copy are out of scope.

## SM1-C restoration and boundary contract

- `restoreMemorialDraft(untrusted)` is asynchronous because it validates any retained
  `choiceUtc` using the current IANA candidate set. It returns `restored`,
  `needs-confirmation` (with editable input and a localized error code), or
  `error` for structurally damaged input. An invalid or stale DST choice is
  removed, never accepted as an implicit earlier/later choice.
- `restoreMemorialInstant(untrusted)` validates a saved resolved record and
  returns its **original UTC**, local civil clock, IANA zone and full-precision
  saved offset. Its arithmetic consistency check does **not** re-resolve
  against today's tzdb. Corrupt or contradictory records are rejected.
- `editMemorialTime(saved)` is deliberately different: it produces a new
  unresolved draft, preserving exact/reference and reference source, while
  discarding old UTC/offset/disambiguation. A subsequent submit uses current
  IANA rules. `changeMemorialLocation` continues to preserve civil time only
  in the memorial creator; explorer location changes still preserve UTC.
- For a fold with one UTC candidate outside the supported range, the creator
  exposes only the eligible candidate and still requires an explicit choice.
  Neither the excluded candidate nor an implicit default can be committed.
  Existing `wallTimeCandidates` callers retain their original range behavior.
- Tests in `tests/memorialTimeRestoration.test.ts` cover archived rule drift,
  both restored fold choices, damaged inputs, the inclusive UTC millisecond
  bounds and local dates on either side of the UTC range. These are **time
  consistency tests**, not independent astronomical validation or tzdb-version
  certification.

## SM2-A circular-projection evidence (November 22, 2026)

The new `projectSkyChart(altitude, azimuth)` pure function takes apparent center altitude and north-clockwise azimuth in **degrees**. Output is a unit-radius disk, centered at the zenith, with screen `x` right and `y` down. The independent analytic anchors in `tests/skyChartProjection.test.ts` establish **S01** (90 degrees at arbitrary azimuth is exactly `(0,0)`) and **S02** (horizon N `(0,-1)`, E `(-1,0)`, S `(0,1)`, W `(1,0)`). Intermediate north positions at altitudes 30, 45, 60 degrees have radii `2/3`, `1/2`, `1/3`; NE at 45 degrees azimuth follows `sqrt(1/2)` per axis at the horizon. These are synthetic analytic geometry cases derived from plan section 6, not JPL/USNO observational measurements.

For finite inputs, altitude `0` degrees is drawable and `[-90,0)` returns `null`; non-finite inputs or altitude outside `[-90,90]` throw `RangeError`. Azimuth wraps modulo 360 degrees. Numerical assertions use 10 decimal places for coordinates and 12 for unit-circle distance; tiny IEEE-754 roundoff at cardinal points is expected. No canvas, timezone, location, camera or browser context is an input. Tests separately cover diagonal quadrants, scale, near-zenith stability, invalid values, and hemisphere-neutral compass orientation.

**Only S01 and S02 have SM2-A implementation evidence.** S03–S07 remain pending: their astronomical observations, daylight/default object-selection policy, catalog/snapshot lifecycle, historical accuracy and new-projection crescent orientation are not verified by these geometry tests. The existing explorer and perspective postcard projection are unchanged. SM2-B is the next separate batch; SM2-C and any drawing/PNG work are later.
